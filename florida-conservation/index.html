    What's counted

    The footprint is the Florida Natural Areas Inventory's Florida Conservation Lands layer, the state's standard list of managed conservation areas. Each area is grouped by who holds title: public agencies, private individuals and companies, nonprofits, or a tribe. Private conservation land is almost entirely under conservation easements, where an agency or land trust holds the development rights; hatching marks easement land in any ownership group. Only one tribal area, the Miccosukee Tribe's 46,000-acre conservation area in the Everglades, is in the inventory; the Seminole Tribe's reservations are not.

    Open water is removed: the Gulf, the Atlantic, Florida Bay and other estuaries outside FDEP’s 1:12,000 shoreline, plus Lake Okeechobee and every lake or river wider than about 1,000 acres. That takes out about 2.1 million acres, mostly in Everglades, Biscayne and the Keys refuges. Marshes and swamps stay in. Florida's land area of 34.6 million acres is the denominator.

    How each acre is dated

    Each piece of the footprint takes the earliest date from parcel records that cover it: deed dates in DEP's state-owned lands inventory (FL‑SOLARIS), closing dates of Florida Forever acquisitions, and establishment dates in USGS PAD‑US 4.1, which carries the National Conservation Easement Database. Where no parcel record exists, the managed area's establishment year is used and marked as estimated.
