    What's counted

    The footprint is the Florida Natural Areas Inventory's Florida Conservation Lands layer, the state's standard list of managed conservation areas. Public means fee title held by a federal, state, water management district or local agency. Private conservation easements are land where an agency or land trust holds the development rights while the owner keeps the land; nearly all of it is privately owned, and every easement is counted here whoever holds title, except the tribal one. Other conservation lands are nonprofit, tribal and private preserves owned outright. The one tribal area in the inventory, the Miccosukee Tribe's 46,000-acre conservation area in the Everglades, comes from a 1982 land-claims settlement rather than an ordinary easement sale, so it is counted with other conservation lands.

    Open water is removed: the Gulf, the Atlantic, Florida Bay and other estuaries outside FDEP’s 1:12,000 shoreline, plus Lake Okeechobee and every lake or river wider than about 1,000 acres. That takes out about 2.1 million acres, mostly in Everglades, Biscayne and the Keys refuges. Marshes and swamps stay in. Florida's land area of 34.6 million acres is the denominator.

    How each acre is dated

    Each piece of the footprint takes the earliest date from parcel records that cover it: deed dates in DEP's state-owned lands inventory (FL‑SOLARIS), closing dates of Florida Forever acquisitions, and establishment dates in USGS PAD‑US 4.1, which carries the National Conservation Easement Database. Where no parcel record exists, the managed area's establishment year is used and marked as estimated.

    How developed land is mapped

    Developed land is every 250 m cell's share of ground covered at least 20% by roofs, roads or pavement in USGS Annual NLCD fractional impervious-surface maps (Collection 1.2), the threshold the National Land Cover Database uses for developed land. Darker cells are more completely built over. Growth from 1985 on follows the satellite maps for 1985, 1990, 1995, 2001, 2006, 2011, 2016 and every year from 2021 through 2025.

    Development before 1985 is timed by the year each building was built, from county property records compiled in HISDAC-US (University of Colorado Boulder). Roads, airports and cells without building records take the average timing of dated cells around them; that is 17% of today's developed area. Demolished buildings drop out of property records, so development before about 1950 is undercounted.
